# Deal_Watch — Hat:Machine Deal Rules

These rules apply only to the active `Hat:Machine` domain. Fishing continues to use `DEAL_RULES.md`.

## Mission gate

For every candidate answer first:

> **Can this machine sew a leather sweatband into a formed felt hat?**

A claim that a machine “sews leather,” “does upholstery,” or is “for hats” is not enough. The formed hat must rotate around the stitch point and the machine must place a controlled seam close to the crown/brim junction without destructive flattening or impractical handling.

## Mission-fit states

- **YES — PURPOSE-BUILT:** documented for hat sweatband installation.
- **YES — PRACTICAL:** usable as-is for the operation despite not being purpose-built.
- **YES — CONVERTIBLE:** suitable fundamental geometry plus a specific, reasonable modification path.
- **MAYBE / VERIFY:** promising, but material facts or geometry remain unverified.
- **NO:** unsuitable operation, stitch, feed, geometry, clearance, or impractical conversion.

A candidate rated **NO** cannot become a deal merely because it is cheap.

## Acquisition tracks

- **Track A — Native:** purpose-built sweatband machines such as verified Singer 103W2 or appropriate Singer 107 subclasses.
- **Track B — Convertible:** cylinder-arm, cylinder-bed, walking-foot cylinder-arm, suitable post-bed, off-the-arm, or useful free-arm industrial machines with credible modifications.
- **Track C — Sleeper:** poorly identified older industrial machines whose photos/model plates reveal favorable geometry and low acquisition cost.

## Required evaluation

For each promising candidate assess:

1. Exact maker, model, and subclass.
2. Original operation, stitch type, feed, needle orientation, and presser-foot arrangement.
3. Whether a formed hat can rotate around the stitch point.
4. Whether the needle can reach sufficiently close to the crown/brim junction.
5. Clearance limits from the casting, arm/cylinder, table, feet, plate, or feed hardware.
6. Suitability for thin leather plus felt without marking or distortion.
7. Slow-speed control.
8. Needed feet, guides, gauges, needles, plates, table changes, motor, reducer, or controls.
9. Whether modifications are bolt-on/fabricatable versus fundamental head/needle-geometry changes.
10. Completeness, condition, parts availability, restoration risk, and serviceability.
11. Asking price, shipping/pickup, modification cost, restoration cost, and all-in acquisition.
12. Remaining versatility for other hat or leather work.

## Conversion discipline

Do not reward hypothetical conversion. A **YES — CONVERTIBLE** rating requires:

- compatible fundamental work geometry;
- an identified modification path;
- economically obtainable or fabricatable parts/attachments;
- a practical all-in cost;
- no dependence on unrealistic casting, head, needle-bar, or feed-system redesign.

Prefer reversible changes involving feet, guides, tables, motors, reducers, needles, plates, and controls.

## Deal Score

Use the shared 1–10 acquisition-value scale:

- **10 — Exceptional / buy immediately**
- **9 — Rare bargain**
- **8 — Strong buy**
- **7 — Interesting**
- **6 — Fair market or useful benchmark**
- **Below 6 — Normally do not surface unless strategically informative**

Score mission fit before price. Then consider delivered cost, modification/restoration cost, exact-model confidence, condition, completeness, seller/transaction risk, serviceability, rarity, and realistic resale/use value.

## Working price behavior

- Purpose-built new machines establish ceilings and benchmarks, not automatic deals.
- A complete, operable purpose-built vintage machine in the hundreds to low-$1,000s may be highly interesting, subject to subclass, completeness, gauges/guides, and restoration risk.
- A $100–$500 sleeper can outrank a scarce native machine when geometry is verified and conversion is specific, economical, and practical.
- Head-only pricing must not be compared with complete table/stand/motor setups without normalizing all-in cost.
- Shipping and rigging count. Local inspection/pickup near ZIP 98053 can materially improve value.

These are provisional behaviors, not fixed thresholds; refine them from observed listings.

## Reporting format

For worthwhile candidates report:

- source and full clickable URL;
- location;
- asking price, shipping/pickup, and estimated all-in cost;
- exact model/subclass confidence;
- seller claim versus independently verified evidence;
- acquisition track and mission-fit state;
- geometry and clearance findings;
- required modification plan and estimated cost;
- condition/completeness/parts risks;
- realistic benchmark or market value;
- Deal Score and concise recommendation;
- questions or photos needed from the seller.

## Known negative examples

- **Juki MB-372 / Z002:** **NO** — dedicated button sewer; wrong operation and work geometry.
- **Grossmann G6:** **NO for installation sewing** when described as an embossing/cutting/decorating tool.
- Straw-braid, binding, embroidery, hemming, or decorative hat machines remain **MAYBE / VERIFY** until the exact sweatband-installation operation is established.

## Notification threshold

Notify only for worthwhile new or meaningfully changed candidates, including:

- a new credible purpose-built, practical, convertible, or sleeper machine;
- a material price drop;
- new photos/model evidence changing mission fit;
- availability or completeness changes making a candidate actionable.

Do not repeatedly alert on unchanged benchmark listings.
