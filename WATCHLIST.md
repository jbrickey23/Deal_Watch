## Watch — Fishing:WorkerRod

Fully qualified: `Deal_Watch:Fishing:WorkerRod`

Mission: find the likely go-to light spinning rod for panfish/trout through finesse and general light-duty bass, complementing existing heavier rods.

Core profile:
- spinning rod;
- Medium-Light strongly preferred;
- Fast preferred; Extra Fast acceptable;
- approximately 6'8"–7'2";
- 2-piece strongly preferred for transport;
- fishes approximately 1/8 oz well, with useful upper range through at least 3/8 oz and preferably 1/2–5/8 oz;
- approximately 4–10 or 6–12 lb line class;
- shorter rear handle preferred;
- continuous/full cork preferred;
- likely 1000–2500 spinning-reel pairing.

Named priority families include Shimano Expride, Zodias, Cumara, Crucial, Poison Adrena; comparable G. Loomis; and Worker-compatible current/prior Fenwick Eagle, HMG, Elite, Walleye, Inshore, and general spinning rods. Search by specifications and photos as well as exact model strings.

Current anchors:
- Fenwick Eagle Walleye `EGLW70ML-FS-2` — value baseline around $99.95 new.
- Fenwick HMG Walleye `HMGW72ML-FS-2` — performance benchmark around $179.95 new.

## Watch — Hat:SweatbandMachine

Fully qualified: `Deal_Watch:Hat:SweatbandMachine`

### Mission question
For every candidate machine, answer first:

> **Can this machine sew a leather sweatband into a felt hat?**

This is application-specific. Merely sewing leather does not qualify a machine. The formed felt hat must be able to rotate around the stitch point while the machine places a controlled seam close to the crown/brim junction without destructive flattening or impractical manipulation.

### Acquisition tracks

#### Track A — Native / purpose-built
Machines designed for hat sweatband insertion or strongly documented for that exact operation. Priority examples include Singer 103W2 and appropriate Singer 107 subclasses / dedicated sweatband machines. Exact subclass must be verified; do not generalize all Singer 107-series machines.

#### Track B — Convertible
Ordinary industrial machines whose fundamental geometry permits the operation and which can become genuinely useful sweatband machines through reasonable changes such as:
- narrow or left-side presser/walking foot;
- custom foot or sweatband/edge guide;
- suitable needle and thread;
- needle plate/feed adjustments where appropriate;
- servo motor and/or speed reduction;
- removable/custom table arrangement;
- knee/foot lift or other control improvements.

Highest-interest convertible classes:
- cylinder-arm / cylinder-bed industrial machines;
- walking-foot cylinder-arm machines;
- suitable post-bed machines;
- off-the-arm or genuinely useful free-arm industrial machines;
- other specialty machines whose work geometry can be demonstrated to fit a formed felt hat.

Named families worth investigating include Juki LS-341/related cylinder-arm designs, Consew 227/227R-class machines, Adler 69-class machines, 441-style cylinder-arm machines, and comparable clones/older machines. Inclusion is for investigation, not automatic qualification.

#### Track C — Sleeper
Cheap, poorly identified, older industrial machines where seller terminology is weak but photos/model plates reveal favorable geometry. This is a high-priority discovery track because a $100–$500 obscure convertible machine may provide much better acquisition value than a scarce purpose-built machine.

### Mission-fit states
- **YES — PURPOSE-BUILT:** documented for hat/sweatband work.
- **YES — PRACTICAL:** non-purpose-built but usable as-is for formed felt-hat sweatband installation.
- **YES — CONVERTIBLE:** fundamental geometry is suitable and a specific, reasonable modification path is identified.
- **MAYBE / VERIFY:** promising geometry but material facts remain unknown.
- **NO:** geometry/function prevents practical sweatband installation or machine is dedicated to an incompatible operation.

### Convertible-machine test
For Track B/C candidates explicitly answer:
1. Can a formed hat physically rotate around the sewing point?
2. Can the needle reach close enough to the crown/brim junction?
3. What limits clearance: head casting, arm/cylinder, presser foot, needle orientation, table, or feed hardware?
4. Is the stitch/feed suitable for thin sweatband leather plus felt?
5. Can foot pressure/feed be controlled without marking or distorting the felt/leather?
6. Can it sew slowly and controllably?
7. Exactly what modifications/attachments would be needed?
8. Are those modifications bolt-on/fabricatable, or would they require impractical changes to fundamental head/needle geometry?
9. What is the all-in acquisition + modification cost?
10. After conversion, does the machine retain useful versatility for other hat/leather operations?

Fundamental head/needle geometry is treated as difficult/impractical to modify. Favor machines where conversion is primarily feet, guides, table, motor/control, needles, or other reversible/fabricatable hardware.

### Required evaluation dimensions
Assess exact maker/model/subclass; original operation; bed/arm geometry; clearance; stitch type; feed; presser-foot and needle arrangement; distance achievable from crown/brim junction; speed control; sweatband-leather capability; parts/needle/foot availability; completeness; operational condition; restoration risk; modification plan and cost; asking/delivered price; market value; and transaction risk.

### Search behavior
Use exact-purpose and broad sleeper queries, including: hat sweatband sewing machine; sweatband machine; millinery machine; Singer 103W2; Singer 107 sweatband; cylinder arm sewing machine; cylinder bed walking foot; Juki LS-341; Consew 227/227R; Adler 69; post bed sewing machine; off the arm sewing machine; free arm industrial sewing machine; vintage industrial sewing machine; leather sewing machine; cobbler/patcher machines where geometry merits inspection.

Search photos/model plates when titles are vague. Do not reject an unknown machine solely because the seller does not mention hats.

### Negative example
**Juki MB-372 / Z002 button sewer:** **NO**. Dedicated button sewer; lacks the continuous-seam function/work geometry needed for sweatband installation.

### Deal behavior
Favor mission fit + low all-in cost + controllability + serviceability. Track B/C machines may outrank scarce native machines when the conversion is credible, inexpensive, reversible, and leaves a more versatile useful machine. Do not reward hypothetical conversion without identifying the actual geometry and modifications required.

### Search geography
Target ZIP **98053**, approximately **500-mile radius** where supported. Shipping-capable national listings may qualify when delivered cost remains compelling.
